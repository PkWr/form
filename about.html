About Form

Form is a small tool based on a publication, 11 ways I can make you up (Visual Identity). The book starts with one letter, a T built from square blocks, and asks how many ways a single form can be remade by moving its parts.

Written and designed by pkwr.

References

Two ideas sit behind it. The first is the Stations of the Cross: one figure, a fixed sequence of stops, each one a pause, and the T standing in for a cross. The second is Paul Klee's way of describing drawing as taking a line for a walk (Pedagogical Sketchbook, 1925). Here a block takes the walk, and its trace is the line left behind.

The rule

A block moves to a new place and the place it left stays marked. Everything in the book comes from that one move, shown in different ways.

The sections

iForm: the T and the variations made by moving one block.

iiDirection: each block is drawn as stripes that run along the line it is joined on.

iiiCumulative Solid: the blocks that moved leave a solid trace behind.

ivCumulative Transparency: the traces build up as tone.

vCumulative Density: the traces build up as texture.

The tool has Form, Cumulative Solid and Direction so far. Transparency and Density are still to come.

Using it

Pick a shape, click the grid to place blocks (click again to remove), choose how many moves, then press Process. You get every variation on a contact sheet and as an animation. Both download as SVG, ready to use as artwork.
